On the K-1 question — the profit %, loss %, and capital % in Item J of Schedule K-1 are allowed to differ, and in syndicated real estate deals they usually do, on purpose. The partnership agreement can allocate income and losses on completely different formulas from each other (and from capital ownership), which is standard practice for a few reasons: real estate partnerships often shift depreciation losses disproportionately toward whichever partner can actually use them (frequently a tax-credit or high-bracket investor), while operating profit or cash flow gets split under a different waterfall tied to preferred returns or promote structures; IRS "substantial economic effect" rules under Reg. 1.704-1(b) explicitly allow special allocations as long as they have real economic substance, so a deal can validly say "losses 99% to Partner A, profits 60/40 between A and B"; and minimum gain chargeback or qualified income offset provisions can force a temporary reallocation in a given year that shows up as a mismatch between the two percentages. None of that is inherently a red flag — it reflects the deal's economics as negotiated, though if you're looking at it for the court case, it's worth checking the actual partnership/operating agreement to confirm the K-1 matches what was agreed.
