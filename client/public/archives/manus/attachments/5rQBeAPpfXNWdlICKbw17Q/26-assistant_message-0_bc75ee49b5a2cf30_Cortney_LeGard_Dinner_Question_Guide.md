# Dinner Question Guide — Cortney LeGard

**Purpose:** Help the family understand Cortney’s companies, interest in the business, ability to proceed, and the public patent docket in a calm, professional way.

> **Use this as a conversation guide, not an interrogation.** Ask the early questions at dinner. Reserve detailed financial documents, formal diligence, and legal review for after both sides agree to proceed under a confidentiality agreement. I am not a lawyer; this is general information, not legal advice.

## A Simple Opening

> “We appreciate you taking the time to meet with us. Our family has built this business carefully, so before we share sensitive information, we would like to understand your companies, your vision for ours, and how you normally approach a transaction.”

## Questions About Her Companies and Experience

| Topic | Question to ask | What it clarifies |
|---|---|---|
| Company portfolio | “Could you walk us through the companies you own or manage today and how they relate to each other?” | Her operating platform and which entities are relevant. |
| Leadership role | “What is your day-to-day role in each company, and who runs the operations and finance functions?” | Whether she is an active operator and who will be involved. |
| Rail 1 | “What have been the biggest lessons from building Rail 1, and how do you manage equipment, staffing, safety, and customer response across field operations?” | Experience in equipment- and labor-intensive services. |
| Petro-Flow | “How does Petro-Flow fit into your overall business strategy, and what parts of the oilfield-services market are most important to you today?” | The strategic role of the adjacent oilfield business. |
| Operating scale | “What types of customers, geographies, and job sizes are your companies focused on today?” | Commercial fit and operating footprint. |
| Team depth | “Who are the key operational and financial leaders you would involve if a transaction moved forward?” | Management bench and execution support. |
| Acquisition history | “Have you acquired or partnered with other companies before? If so, what made those arrangements work well?” | Transaction and integration experience. |

## Questions About Her Interest in Your Family’s Company

| Topic | Question to ask | What it clarifies |
|---|---|---|
| Motivation | “What specifically interested you in our company?” | Whether the interest is strategic and well informed. |
| Strategic fit | “How would our services, equipment, people, and customer relationships fit with your current businesses?” | Her thinking about synergies and value creation. |
| Long-term vision | “If you acquired the company, what would you hope it looks like in three to five years?” | Growth plan and cultural alignment. |
| Employees | “How do you generally approach retaining employees and preserving the operating culture of a family-run company?” | People and continuity priorities. |
| Family role | “Would you expect family members to remain involved after a closing? If so, in what capacity and for how long?” | Transition expectations. |
| Customers and vendors | “How would you protect customer relationships and vendor continuity during a transition?” | Commercial-risk awareness. |
| Brand and legacy | “Would you see value in retaining the company name, leadership team, and local presence?” | Respect for the family’s legacy and local goodwill. |

## Questions About Process, Authority, and Funding

These should be asked only if the conversation is clearly serious. You do **not** need to reveal detailed financial statements, customer lists, or pricing information during dinner.

| Topic | Question to ask | What it clarifies |
|---|---|---|
| Buyer entity | “Which legal entity would be the buyer, and who would have final authority to make an offer and sign a letter of intent?” | Who is actually proposing to buy the company. |
| Decision process | “Besides you, who would participate in evaluating, approving, and closing a transaction?” | Decision-makers and timing. |
| Financing path | “If we both decide to continue, what is your normal process for demonstrating funding capacity or proof of funds?” | Seriousness without demanding bank statements at dinner. |
| Structure | “Would you envision an all-cash transaction, third-party financing, seller financing, or another structure?” | High-level deal structure. |
| Timing | “What timeline would you have in mind for an initial offer, diligence, and a potential closing?” | Execution readiness. |
| Confidentiality | “Would you be comfortable starting with a mutual confidentiality agreement before we exchange detailed company information?” | Controlled disclosure process. |
| Advisors | “Which attorney, CPA, lender, or transaction advisor would you expect to involve if the conversation advances?” | Professional readiness and process discipline. |

## Questions About the Public Patent Docket

A public federal docket for **CP Energy Services, Inc. v. Petro-Flow Control, LLC d/b/a Green Machine et al.** identifies Cortney LeGard, Petro-Flow Control LLC, and Petro Environmental Solutions LLC d/b/a Green Machine as defendants. The publicly available docket summary shows a settlement notice and a subsequent entry titled **“Stipulated Permanent Injunction and Dismissal with Prejudice.”** [1]

Do **not** ask this at the beginning of dinner. If the conversation becomes serious, ask privately and matter-of-factly, without suggesting wrongdoing or debating the legal merits.

> “We saw a public docket involving Petro-Flow and CP Energy. We understand that legal matters can be nuanced, and we are not trying to make assumptions. Before we move into a formal transaction process, could you give us the high-level context and let us know whether there are any continuing obligations relevant to a potential acquisition?”

| Topic | Neutral question to ask | Why it is appropriate |
|---|---|---|
| High-level context | “Could you explain the business context of that matter at a high level?” | Gives her a fair opportunity to explain without accusing. |
| Resolution | “Our understanding is that the public docket reflects a settlement and stipulated permanent injunction. Is that accurate, and is the matter fully resolved?” | Tests whether her account aligns with the public record. |
| Continuing restrictions | “Are there any current restrictions, obligations, licenses, or compliance requirements that could affect operating, acquiring, financing, or integrating a service business?” | Identifies potential transaction relevance without drawing legal conclusions. |
| Financial impact | “Are there any remaining payment, insurance, indemnity, or reporting obligations that a buyer’s due diligence team should understand?” | Frames the question around disclosure and process. |
| Documentation | “If we move forward after a confidentiality agreement, would your counsel be willing to provide the final order and explain any continuing obligations?” | Appropriately defers legal interpretation to lawyers. |
| Other matters | “Are there any other active claims, injunctions, disputes, or contractual restrictions that you believe a seller should know about before entering a formal process?” | Seeks a complete, professional disclosure. |

## Phrases to Avoid

| Avoid saying | Use instead |
|---|---|
| “Were you found guilty?” | “Could you give us the high-level context and current status?” |
| “Is your company in trouble?” | “Are there any continuing obligations that could affect a future transaction?” |
| “Can you prove you have the money?” | “What is your normal process for demonstrating funding capacity after mutual interest is confirmed?” |
| “Why should we trust you?” | “What would a thoughtful, well-run process look like from your perspective?” |
| “What happened with the lawsuit?” | “We saw a public docket and would appreciate the high-level context if we move toward formal diligence.” |

## How to Close the Conversation

> “Thank you for being open with us. We will discuss the conversation as a family and with our advisors. If there is mutual interest, the next step for us would be a confidentiality agreement, a defined information-sharing process, and a clearer understanding of buyer entity, financing, and transaction timing.”

**Do not commit to a price, exclusivity, or sensitive information disclosure at dinner.** Have counsel review any confidentiality agreement, letter of intent, funding documentation, or court order before the family signs or relies on it.
